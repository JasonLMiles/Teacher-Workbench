# Instructional-assistant small groups — Teacher Workbench 1.3.0

`tw-instructional-assistant-small-groups` turns a lesson, curriculum reference or conversational goal into a complete activity an instructional assistant can lead with appropriate preparation and planned teacher review. It supports California K–12 across subjects; there is no assumed grade level or personal classroom profile.

## Choose the adult support mode

| Mode | Best fit for this task | What the assistant receives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fully scripted | New to the content or routine | Plain-language primer, preparation self-check with answers, exact say/show/do directions, worked keys and specific error responses. |
| Guided | Familiar with the content; benefits from a clear sequence | Compact run sheet, targeted prompts, acceptable responses and decision branches. |
| Flexible within the teacher's plan | Demonstrated knowledge of this content and facilitation routine | Shared learning/evidence criteria, complete task bank and defined choices for pacing, prompts and extension. |

These are proposed preparation options, not certified expertise levels. Adult experience changes the guidance provided; it does not lower student expectations. Ask for parallel versions when assistants rotate: each uses the same task IDs and evidence criteria.

## What a full packet contains

The consistent **TW-IA 1.3** format has ten sections: assignment/quick start; content primer/rehearsal; timed run sheet; actual student tasks/materials; separate adult keys; scaffolds/language/access supports; if-this-then-that decisions; individual check/brief record; teacher handback/classroom connection; and teacher source/change notes. A required school template takes precedence.

Scaffolds include actual cues, visuals, vocabulary models, oral rehearsal and response options. New material receives explicit teaching. Temporary help is adjusted using a fresh comparable response; documented accommodations remain available. Record first attempts, coached work and fresh checks separately. Keep absent or unobserved evidence distinct from an incorrect answer.

The assistant receives routes for common misconceptions, no start, a correct answer with weak explanation, fast/slow work rates, several students stuck, frustration, missing materials and an unfamiliar response. Every learner has a prepared task while the adult helps someone else. A supplied fallback and extension reduce avoidable interruptions. The teacher gets a short record of learning evidence, support used, delivery issues and proposed follow-up.

## Example requests

> Use tw-instructional-assistant-small-groups. Turn this uploaded lesson into a 20-minute group for five learners. The assistant is new to this topic. Give a five-minute preparation guide, exact teaching script, student tasks, worked answers, likely-error responses, oral language supports, a fresh individual check and a brief teacher handback. We have paper and counters. I will be teaching nearby; use the classroom routines I supplied.

> Create guided and fully scripted run cards for two assistants who rotate through the same group. Keep the learning target, student task IDs and evidence criteria consistent. Show what can be adjusted without interrupting me and what belongs in our check-in.

> Adapt this group for multilingual learners while preserving its content goal and the documented response accommodations. Write the actual language models and practice turns. Include a way to reduce temporary prompts after students show the strategy on a new example.

## Evidence and responsibilities

[EEF's 2025 teaching-assistant guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants) informs preparation, classroom connection and scaffolding toward independence. Its evidence context is England; it is not a California requirement or a WWC evidence rating. The [full report](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/deployment_of_teaching_assistants_-_guidance_report_v1.1.0.pdf), [scaffolding tool](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/scaffolding_framework.pdf) and [partnership tool](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/effective_teacher_%E2%80%93_teaching_assistant_partnerships.pdf) are linked for review. Generated activity scripts and decision thresholds are practical proposals, not tested interventions.

Select relevant WWC guidance for the specific subject, grade and purpose. For example, the [WWC elementary mathematics intervention guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/26) rates systematic instruction, mathematical language and representations as strong-evidence recommendations within its K–6 scope. That rating does not validate every assistant-led activity.

[California Education Code §45344(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EDC&sectionNum=45344) permits aide duties outside the teacher's physical presence while retaining teacher responsibility for instruction and supervision; aides may not assign grades. Use actual school procedures, required supports and relevant program training. The skill distinguishes routine decisions, issues for planned review and situations requiring prompt contact. It does not invent local procedures.

## Files and optional technology

Ask for printable assistant/student pages, a paper observation grid, or editable documents. Sheets and Canva requests route through their existing skills and actual connected capabilities; an available editable file fallback is labeled accurately. Student AI is optional. Current sources, including law or AI research when relevant, are checked at use.

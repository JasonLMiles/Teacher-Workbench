# Grade 3 area and perimeter exit ticket — corrected resource

**Status:** Proposed original classroom material; reviewed against selected Teacher Workbench criteria on 2026-09-29. This is a brief formative check, not a disability screener, placement test, or demonstration of durable mastery.

## T1. Teacher preparation

**Target:** Distinguish the area of a rectangle in square units from its perimeter in units, and explain what each measure counts.

**Use:** After students have explored area and perimeter. Allow about 5–7 minutes, plus required time accommodations. This timing is a planning estimate, not a speed criterion. Give each student the student section only, blank paper, and a pencil. Make familiar square tiles, blank grid paper, and accessible response tools available as needed; record which tools and prompts were used.

**Access:** Preserve documented IEP/504 and language supports. Read directions verbatim when needed. Students may draw, write, point to a self-created model, dictate to an adult who records their words, or use their established communication system. The teacher records each student's own reasoning. Handwriting, spelling, English fluency, and response speed are not scored targets. Reading the task or scribing a response does not automatically make the mathematics prompted. Distinguish access help from a hint that supplies the method, unit, or answer. If a support supplies part of the target, keep the support and mark that part as prompted; gather another appropriate opportunity rather than removing a required accommodation. CAST's communication guidance supports varied modes when the mode itself is not the goal. [S5]

**Optional language support:** To help organize an explanation, offer “I found ___ by ___. This measures ___.” The frame leaves the operation, number, unit, and meaning to the student. Record its use. Accept a student's explanation in a familiar language through established school language support where available; mathematical reasoning is the target, not English proficiency. Do not translate a student's response beyond what can be verified. During subsequent practice, invite the learner to explain without the optional frame when ready; never withdraw required supports to claim independence.

## S1. Student ticket

Name or private classroom ID: ____________________  Date: ____________________

Show your own thinking. You may draw, write, or explain to your teacher.

A rectangle is **3 units tall and 5 units wide**.

1. What is its **area**? Circle one answer.

   A. 8 square units  
   B. 15 square units  
   C. 16 square units

2. What is its **perimeter**? Give a number and its unit.

   My answer: ______________________________________________________

3. Draw or explain how you found **both** answers. Explain what your area measures and what your perimeter measures.

   My thinking: ____________________________________________________

   ________________________________________________________________

   ________________________________________________________________

   Use the back or another sheet if you want more room for a drawing.

## T2. Key and accepted reasoning

- **Item 1:** B, **15 square units**. For example, a rectangle filled without gaps or overlaps by 3 rows of 5 unit squares contains 15 unit squares: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15, or 3 × 5 = 15. A rotated arrangement of 5 rows of 3 is equivalent.
- **Item 2:** **16 units**. The four side lengths total 3 + 5 + 3 + 5 = 16. Equivalent strategies include 2 × 3 + 2 × 5 or 2 × (3 + 5).
- **Item 3:** The explanation/model should connect area to covering the interior with unit squares and perimeter to the total length around the outside. Accept correct drawings, counting, repeated addition, multiplication, gestures, or spoken explanations that make those meanings clear. A correct formula alone is evidence of a procedure; it does not establish the distinction in meaning. The multiple-choice item supplies area units, so it does not independently show that a student can generate the correct unit.

These are author-created examples, not actual student responses.

## T3. Record evidence without a total grade

Use one private row per student. Record the response and support conditions. This resource produces a formative profile, not points, a percent grade, a disability label, or a tier assignment.

| Evidence | Demonstrated on this check (D) | Partly demonstrated / revisit (P) | Not yet shown (N) | No interpretable evidence (NE) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Area | Gives the expected area and compatible reasoning/model (initial ticket: B, 15 square units) | Correct answer with absent/unclear reasoning, or appropriate area model with a counting error | Interpretable reasoning treats area as side lengths added or another incompatible measure | Blank or response cannot be interpreted after an access check |
| Perimeter | Gives the expected perimeter, linear unit, and compatible boundary strategy (initial ticket: 16 units) | Correct number with missing/incorrect unit, or valid boundary strategy with an arithmetic error | Interpretable reasoning uses an area procedure or another incompatible measure | Blank or uninterpretable response |
| Difference in meaning | Explains/models interior square coverage versus total boundary length and distinguishes their units | Explains one meaning, or both meanings with unclear units | Interpretable explanation conflates the measures | No interpretable explanation/model |

If a response is ambiguous, use a neutral clarification such as “Tell me what you counted” and note it before assigning a code. Add **AP** if an answer, operation, definition, or unit was supplied by an adult. Record a row with successful prompted performance as **P-AP**, not D; retain other rows actually demonstrated by the student. Preserve the work and the exact help given. Also record required access supports and optional frames, which do not by themselves imply AP. Record the area choice separately from the explanation.

**Class summary template:** “Roster: ___; absent: ___; present but not offered the check: ___; offered the check: ___. For each evidence row, D: ___, P: ___, N: ___, NE: ___. Of these, ___ received an answer/method prompt. Observed response modes/access supports: ___. Next action: ___.”

For each evidence row, D + P + N + NE must equal the number offered the check. Include P-AP within P and also report its count separately. Students absent or not offered the check are recorded separately without D/P/N/NE codes for this administration. Keep those counts visible so offered + absent + present-not-offered equals the roster. A blank offered paper remains NE in that denominator. If reporting a rate, say “___ D out of ___ offered,” state prompt conditions, and report missing counts beside it. Do not turn NE into demonstrated failure or erase it. No response counts are supplied in this resource.

## T4. Interpret responses tentatively and act

Read the explanation with the answer. Each possible pattern below is a hypothesis to check, not a diagnosis.

| Observed evidence | Neutral follow-up and next teaching move |
| --- | --- |
| A, 8 square units | Ask “Show what you counted.” If the student added only 3 and 5, use the short model in T5 to contrast a side-length sum with complete square coverage. |
| C, 16 square units | Ask “Show what the 16 counts.” If the explanation traces the outside, contrast that boundary length with the interior unit squares using T5. |
| B with weak or missing explanation | Ask the student to show a model or explain the rows and squares. Do not assume a correct choice rules out guessing or confusion. |
| Correct values but swapped/missing units | Have the student point to one square unit and one length unit in the T5 model, then explain what each counts. |
| Blank, ambiguous, or conflicting work | Check access, time, directions, and response mode privately; offer an equivalent accessible opportunity. Treat lack of evidence as uncertainty, not inability. |
| Both quantities and meanings demonstrated | Offer the extension in T6, then check the same target again on a later task before making a broader mastery claim. |

A class pattern helps choose a whole-class model or a temporary small group; examine each student's record. Do not declare every student proficient because a class percentage crosses a threshold. This ticket cannot diagnose a disability or determine Tier 3 placement. Persistent concerns belong in the school's established problem-solving process with multiple sources of evidence; existing supports continue. This distinction is consistent with CDE's description of MTSS as a data-informed, team-based system. [S6]

## T5. Brief reteaching and recheck

**Teacher-led model, about 3–5 minutes:** Use twelve familiar unit square tiles to build a 3-by-4 rectangle with no gaps or overlaps. Point to one tile's face as one square unit and one edge as one unit of length. Count 3 rows of 4 for an area of **12 square units**. Trace the outer edges only; the perimeter is 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 = **14 units**. Ask the learner to explain why inside tile edges are not added to the perimeter. Support the explanation with “I found ___ by ___. This measures ___.” Use the frame only as needed, preserving required supports. This proposed model connects concrete representations and language; it is not an empirically validated intervention package. [S4, S8]

**Fresh recheck:** “A rectangle is 2 units tall and 5 units wide. Find its area and perimeter. Give their units and show or explain what each answer measures.” Give blank paper or the student's established tools. Do not show the key. Allow about 3–4 minutes or required additional time.

**Recheck key:** Area **10 square units**, using 2 rows of 5 squares; perimeter **14 units**, using 2 + 5 + 2 + 5. Use the same T3 evidence categories and record support conditions.

**Decision:** If the recheck demonstrates both measures and the distinction under documented access conditions without answer/method prompting, return to core practice and sample the target on another day. If the same confusion remains, provide another brief targeted lesson and review the actual strategy and access conditions. No automatic tier change follows. If many students share the same verified error, reteach the distinction to the class while giving students who have shown it appropriate practice. The collect–interpret–teach–recheck cycle is consistent with WWC's data-use recommendation 1, rated **Minimal Evidence**; no precise regrouping threshold is prescribed by that guide. [S7]

## T6. Extension and next-day planning

**Extension:** “Draw two rectangles that both have area 12 square units but different perimeters. Label and explain.”

**One possible key:** A 3-by-4 rectangle has area 12 square units and perimeter 14 units; a 2-by-6 rectangle has area 12 square units and perimeter 16 units. Accept other correct pairs, such as 1-by-12 and 3-by-4, with correct labels and reasoning.

Plan the next lesson from the evidence and the class's established sequence. This check does not establish readiness for volume. “Volume” is ambiguous: grade 3 liquid-volume measurement and grade 5 geometric volume are different targets. No claim about a particular learner's readiness for either is warranted by this ticket. [S9, S4]

## T7. Alignment and source notes for teachers

This brief check samples **CA 3.MD.7.b** (rectangle area) and part of **CA 3.MD.8** (known-side perimeter and distinguishing linear from area measure), using the unit-square meaning in **CA 3.MD.5.a**. It does not assess the full standards. The initial ticket does not require every student to tile and connect tiling to multiplication, so it is not a complete check of **3.MD.7.a**. The extension samples the same-area/different-perimeter portion of 3.MD.8. These alignment claims describe task demand, not observed learning. [S1–S3, S10]

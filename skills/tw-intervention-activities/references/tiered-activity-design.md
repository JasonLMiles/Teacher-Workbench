# Tiered activity design

Use the teacher's observed evidence to select a narrow, actionable target. A low overall score is a signal to investigate; it is not an explanation. Read actual responses, prerequisite demands, instruction received, language/access conditions and implementation before naming the next teaching step.

| Support context | Activity design | Evidence and coordination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1: core support | Preserve grade-level instruction; provide an explicit model, varied representations, active responses, feedback and flexible in-class practice. Reteach a widespread misconception through stronger core instruction. | A brief class/group check, access review and classroom follow-up. Tier 1 can include small groups; group size alone does not define a tier. |
| Tier 2: supplemental | Build a sequenced activity for a shared target in addition to core learning. Include cumulative review, models, repeated response opportunities, feedback and transfer. Preserve an adopted intervention's verified conditions. | Baseline, comparable progress measures, delivery record, review window and response rules matched to the program/measure. |
| Tier 3: intensive individualized support | Start with the learner's evidence and intervention history. Adapt an appropriate intervention platform in a documented DBI cycle. Supply a specific individualized activity, rationale and next diagnostic question. | Authorized team review, frequent appropriate monitoring, implementation checks and individually justified adjustments. A worksheet or smaller group alone is not Tier 3. |

This table is an original design aid informed by [California MTSS](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/cr/ri/mtsscomprti2.asp), the [MTSS Center's tier descriptions](https://mtss4success.org/essential-components/multi-level-prevention-system) and [NCII's DBI process](https://intensiveintervention.org/data-based-individualization). Tiers are support configurations, not diagnoses. Intensive instruction and disability services may overlap, but one does not automatically establish the other. Local student plans and referral obligations still apply.

## From evidence to an actual activity

1. Write **observed response → plausible skill/access hypothesis → target → activity → fresh check**. Keep competing explanations if evidence is ambiguous.
2. Select an applicable [WWC guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguides) or a reviewed program; record the precise recommendation, evidence level, population and delivery conditions. Distinguish established practices from this newly authored activity.
3. Supply a worked example, guided and independent tasks, exact error correction, response supports, a key and a transfer application. Provide enough content for the stated session, including what happens if learners progress faster or remain stuck.
4. Name the change in intensity and why it addresses the hypothesis. [NCII's intensity taxonomy](https://intensiveintervention.org/implementation-intervention/taxonomy-intervention-intensity) considers evidence strength, practice/feedback opportunities, alignment, transfer, explicitness/comprehensiveness, supporting academic/behavioral needs and ongoing individualization. Do not assume “longer” always means better.
5. Use [NCII progress-monitoring guidance](https://intensiveintervention.org/data-based-individualization/progress-monitoring) to plan the measure, goal, collection/review frequency and adaptation record. A generated exit ticket may be useful formative evidence without being a validated progress-monitoring instrument.

## Design example — synthetic, not an adopted program

Observed: learners solve 2 groups of 3 as 2+3. Target: distinguish group count, amount per group and total. Tier 1 proposal: a short whole-class model and contrast with matching pictures. Tier 2 proposal: supplemental guided equal-group construction with cumulative examples and immediate correction. Tier 3 proposal: use individual error/progress evidence to isolate which representation or counting/grouping step fails, adapt the intervention platform and check a new example. The implemented response must supply the actual tasks, keys and schedule, not stop at these descriptions. Link each version back to current multiplication learning and avoid a permanent low group.

# tw-student-reflection-tracking

Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) every time and [coordination](references/coordination.md) when routing work. Verify sources at use; apply only the foundations relevant to this task. Honor teacher AI preferences and preserve documented supports.


## Inputs
Use grade/age, learning goal, student-facing audience, medium, frequency and available evidence. Accept freeform conversation or source plans. Default to a private individual sheet with one target and short review cycle. Do not request student names or a class roster to make a blank template.

## Workflow
1. State a concrete target and student-friendly success criteria. Decide whether the purpose is reflection, evidence of learning, habit tracking or behavior self-monitoring. Keep them distinct; confidence, compliance and completion are not mastery measures.
2. Design a short plan → monitor → evaluate routine embedded in the subject task. Supply actual age-appropriate prompts: what am I trying, what evidence can I show, what worked, what will I change, and what help do I need? Offer oral, drawing, checklist or home-language routes when appropriate. Do not require sensitive personal disclosure.
3. Create a usable reflection page using [student reflection](assets/student-reflection.md), plus a [progress record](assets/student-progress-log.csv) when longitudinal data helps. Include date, target, evidence/task link, measure and units, success criterion when established, support conditions, student's interpretation, next action and review date. See [measurement and privacy](references/tracking-rules.md).
4. For quantities, define numerator/denominator or duration and observation window. Preserve blanks, absence and not observed separately from measured zero. Avoid incompatible-score averages, false precision, permanent ability labels or a single composite across unrelated subjects. Chart comparable observations and show changed tasks/support conditions.
5. Model one clearly fictional completed example, then give a blank reusable version. Provide a brief teacher conferencing routine and a next-step rule labeled as a proposal. For behavior routines coordinate with `tw-behavior-pbis`; for scaffolds keep required accommodations while checking independent use of temporary prompts.
6. For native spreadsheets use `tw-google-sheets`; for designed pages use teaching-aids/Canva production. Keep each student's private data separate from other learners' visible views. Blank templates can be shared as requested; do not publish populated student data or create a class leaderboard by default.

## Deliver
Return actual student-facing prompts and fields, a blank reusable sheet, a labeled model entry, and a concise teacher guide with verified pedagogical sources. Include field definitions and scoring limits when numeric. If asked for an artifact, produce it with the available tools rather than only proposing its contents.

## Examples and limits
“Make a grade 4 fraction goal/reflection sheet” → concrete criterion, evidence example, support field and next action. “Let students graph reading growth” → define the reading measure and comparable conditions before plotting. “Rank all students by effort” → explain the interpretation/privacy problem and propose private, observable target tracking. Do not use self-ratings to diagnose, award eligibility or automatically determine grades.

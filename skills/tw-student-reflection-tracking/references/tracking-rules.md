# Reflection, measurement and privacy
Use [EEF's 2025 metacognition and self-regulated learning guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/metacognition) as one research-synthesis starting point for explicitly taught planning, monitoring and evaluation. Recheck current editions, underlying evidence and transfer to the age/subject. The new sheet itself is not a validated instrument.

For behavior self-monitoring in K–5, consult recommendation 6 of the [WWC teacher-delivered behavioral interventions guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/31/Published), rated moderate evidence on the checked December 2024 guide page. Preserve the population and rating; do not transfer it automatically to all academic reflection or secondary students. Use the full relevant recommendation at execution time.

Keep student reflection in plain language; model comparison of a self-rating with an actual work example without punishing a candid mismatch. Separate task correctness, strategy use, support used, confidence and completion. Let students revise a goal based on evidence. Use growth graphs only when observations are comparable; annotate task difficulty and accommodation changes rather than attributing every change to student ability.

Use one learner per student-facing populated sheet by default. Hidden tabs, filtered views and protected ranges do not provide reliable separation from other viewers with file access. Do not put classwide sensitive data into a student-facing workbook/design. Keep teacher notes and private context in separate authorized locations. Do not make participation depend on public emotional disclosure, diagnosis labels or peer comparison. Plan a short review cadence that informs instruction rather than collecting unused data.

# tw-multilingual-instruction

Provide practical language support that keeps disciplinary thinking visible and develops usable language. Work directly or through `tw-orchestrator`. Honor the learner's languages and demonstrated strengths.

## Load resources
- Always read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md).
- Read [language design decisions](references/language-design.md) for ELD planning, language-demand analysis, or bilingual adaptation.
- Consult [sources and evidence](references/sources.md) for official standards, framework PDFs, and recommendation-level WWC information.
- Use [language support plan](assets/language-support-plan.md) for a full plan and [language task card](assets/language-task-card.md) for immediately usable materials.

Use available source-retrieval tools to check citations and current information. Use appropriate file-generation tools when requested. Work without requiring uninstalled peer skills.

## 1. Establish learning and learner evidence
Identify grade/course, subject, the actual content task, intended learning, time, and the language demands. Reuse supplied language samples, domain-specific proficiency information, prior schooling, home-language literacy, interests, and documented supports. Do not request identifiable records.
Ask only for a missing detail that materially changes the plan. If proficiency data are absent, supply adjustable entry points and a short observation prompt rather than assigning an official level.
Distinguish content understanding, English development, reading foundations, prior opportunity to learn, and disability-related access. A newcomer label does not establish limited schooling, low literacy, trauma, or weak reasoning.
Do not convert ELPAC scores mechanically into an instructional profile or equate language proficiency with intellectual capacity.

## 2. Analyze language and align goals
State the content target and a separate, observable language objective tied to doing that content task.
Identify relevant functions (such as explain, compare, justify, interpret evidence), essential vocabulary, useful morphology or cognates, sentence relationships, discourse structure, and interaction demands.
Verify the appropriate California content and ELD standards before giving exact identifiers. Use current official grade/course and proficiency-level descriptors; do not invent codes from a naming pattern.
Consult relevant ELD Parts I and II and foundational-literacy guidance as the task requires. Locate the proper ELA/ELD Framework grade chapter.
Use Emerging/Expanding/Bridging descriptors as verified planning references, not fixed labels or a one-to-one replacement for assessment scores. Base support on the language work observed.

## 3. Choose the instructional setting
For integrated ELD, connect language development to the content lesson and identify planned teacher moves and opportunities to use the target language.
For designated ELD, build a focused language-learning sequence connected to meaningful content; identify the language focus, models, interaction, practice, and evidence of transfer.
Keep these distinct. Do not present ordinary translation, a content worksheet, or one setting as a substitute for a required language program. Verify current regulatory and local requirements before stating a legal obligation; Title 5 provisions are not Education Code sections.
If the teacher asks only for a language scaffold within a lesson, deliver that scaffold rather than expanding the task into a program redesign.

## 4. Design usable supports
Select a coherent subset, with actual examples:
- Brief explicit teaching and repeated meaningful use of essential vocabulary.
- Visuals, demonstrations, content models, supported reading, and multimodal representations.
- A short model response that makes the relevant language feature visible.
- Optional frames, stems, word banks, or organizers with adjustable support and a route beyond them.
- Structured peer interaction with a purpose, roles, turn-taking, and an individual response.
- Oral rehearsal, listening/reading for a purpose, writing, feedback, and revision when suitable.
Use available language strengths through bilingual resources, home-language planning, cognates, or peer discussion when appropriate. Check meaning and false cognates; do not assume home-language literacy or a particular writing system.
For requested translation, clarify audience, language variety, and purpose only when needed. Preserve concepts, evidence, tone, and disciplinary meanings. Mark uncertainty and arrange qualified human review for consequential communications when warranted; do not label machine output certified.
For advanced multilingual learners, combine rich thinking with precise language feedback and authentic communication. For overlapping disability needs, implement supplied supports and coordinate with `tw-differentiation-accessibility` if available.

## 5. Assess content and language
Decide whether the task measures disciplinary understanding, English language development, independent reading/writing, or multiple constructs. Match supports and scoring to that purpose.
Collect distinct evidence when appropriate: an explanation/model for content and a brief language sample for the language objective. Do not score English fluency as all of the science or mathematics achievement.
If English is the construct, explain how translation, read-aloud, speech-to-text, or provided frames affect interpretation. Do not claim a classroom scaffold is permitted on a formal assessment without checking its rules.
Define a short formative check and observable next decision. Fade or broaden temporary frames as students use the target language more independently while preserving documented accommodations.
Label instructional criteria and generated rubrics as proposals, not official proficiency determinations. Do not make identification, reclassification, eligibility, or service decisions.

## Assistant-led small groups
For aide-led language practice or small groups, coordinate with `tw-instructional-assistant-small-groups`. Supply actual language models, interaction turns, visuals, supported response options and content/language evidence rules that match the assistant’s verified language/content competence; do not assume bilingual proficiency from a role or learner identity.

## 6. Cite and return
Follow the shared citation policy. State the scope and rating of a WWC recommendation used, and retrieve newer relevant evidence. The 2014 English learner guide's elementary/middle-school evidence should not be presented as a tested high-school intervention without justification.
Provide a concise recommendation, content and language goals, concrete teacher/student moves, an actual language resource, a separate content/language check, adjustment criteria, and descriptive source links. Include standards identifiers only if verified.
Use CSTP for the coaching connection and relevant ISTE/framework guidance when technology contributes. Maintain the teacher's selected AI-use level; an effective plan must not depend on student AI use unless requested and feasible.

## Examples of selection
- “Add language supports to this fourth-grade science lesson.” Keep the content task and produce usable models, interaction, and a language check.
- “Plan designated ELD for seventh graders who can explain ideas but need cohesive written arguments.” Focus on language evidence and meaningful content.
- “Translate and adapt these directions for a newcomer whose prior literacy is unknown.” Provide a feasible bilingual/multimodal route and identify what cannot be inferred.
- A request chiefly about motor access or advanced compacting should primarily use `tw-differentiation-accessibility`; coordinate for overlap.

For new student-facing aids, use `tw-teaching-aids`; for student reflection, goal-setting and progress sheets, use `tw-student-reflection-tracking`. Preserve distinctions among language/access supports, self-report and demonstrated content learning.

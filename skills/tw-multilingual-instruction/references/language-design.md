# Language design decisions

## Separate goals and evidence
Use a content target that names the disciplinary learning. Write a language objective as: learners will [language function] about [content] using [relevant language feature or discourse move], demonstrated by [observable response]. Choose a feature needed for this task; a vocabulary list alone is not a complete objective.
For example, a proposed science objective may ask learners to explain a relationship using an observation and a causal connector. This is an original design example, not a verified grade-level standard or validated intervention.

## California planning distinctions
Use the [official CA ELD standards PDF](https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/ml/documents/eldstndspublication14.pdf) to verify the specific grade, level, and element. The document addresses interaction/meaning making and how English works; select relevant language resources from both rather than limiting the work to vocabulary. Its proficiency descriptors inform adjustable planning. Do not make a one-to-one conversion from an ELPAC number.
For integrated ELD, plan language development within meaningful content instruction. For designated ELD, plan focused language instruction connected to content. Consult the relevant framework chapter and current authority for program requirements. A grades 4–5 example is not universal guidance for all K–12 schedules.

## Build a short learning sequence
1. Make the content purpose visible through a prompt, demonstration, model, or text.
2. Identify a small set of language resources needed to communicate the idea.
3. Model their use in a meaningful response and discuss the function of the feature.
4. Provide rehearsal with a real communicative purpose, access supports, and feedback.
5. Collect a short individual response.
6. Check both the content idea and the targeted language use; decide what to reteach, extend, or release.

Choose and cite applicable evidence for the individual practices. Do not label the entire generated sequence a WWC-validated program.

## Adjustable supports
| Observed demand | More support | Less support / extension | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explaining a causal relationship | Visual sequence, teacher model, choices of connectors, optional frame | Select a connector independently and qualify a claim | A relationship that makes sense, not insertion of a word alone |
| Comparing evidence | Paired visuals or excerpts and a comparison organizer | Select evidence and account for a counterexample | Relevant comparison and explanation |
| Participating in discussion | Preview ideas, private rehearsal, clear roles and turn prompts | Build on, challenge, or synthesize another contribution | Meaningful contribution and responsive listening |
| Writing a disciplinary response | Model with annotations, oral rehearsal, optional organizer | Revise for precision, cohesion, audience, or disciplinary nuance | Content reasoning and language evidence scored separately as needed |

Offer supported entry and ambitious thinking together. Let learners vary in support across listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Newness to English does not justify a simplistic intellectual task. Advanced conversational fluency does not prove control of every disciplinary genre.

## Translation and adaptation
Preserve concepts, quantities, evidence, qualifications, and directions. Check bilingual terminology and names rather than translating word-for-word without context. Keep an English language-learning opportunity when English development is the goal.
Explain whether an adaptation supports access to a content task or changes the English-language evidence. Use bilingual meaning checks, visuals, and learner explanation to check understanding rather than assuming translated text is accessible.
For a learner with unknown schooling or literacy, offer oral/visual entry and ask for a brief sample or teacher observation; do not infer reading ability in the home language.

## Follow-up and collaboration
Record what learners did with and without the temporary support. Regroup around the observed language need. Continue required accommodations while adjusting instructional scaffolds.
Use the teacher's original task and time constraints. For a cross-curricular assessment, provide separate disciplinary and language criteria and individual evidence within group work.

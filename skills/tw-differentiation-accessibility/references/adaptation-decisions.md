# Adaptation decision guide
Use these as design decisions to justify with task evidence and appropriate current sources, not as universal efficacy claims.

| Situation | Useful design response | Evidence and scoring check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner can explain an idea orally but struggles to write it | Model planning; provide an organizer and oral rehearsal; use documented assistive support | Decide whether the task measures the concept, composition, transcription, or several separately |
| Reading is blocking access to science/social studies ideas | Offer a supported original, audio/read-aloud where appropriate, vocabulary/glossary, visuals, and chunked evidence | These may support content access without providing evidence of independent decoding |
| Independent decoding is the target | Keep the reading demand necessary to that target; use appropriate instruction and separate practice supports | Audio support changes the evidence of independent decoding; record the condition |
| Writing and content are both targets | Collect separate evidence for each and provide suitable supports | Score disciplinary understanding separately from written language and transcription where justified |
| Relevant mastery is already demonstrated | Reduce redundant practice; design a deeper or advanced application and an observable product | Record what evidence justified compacting; maintain challenge rather than assigning more of the same |
| Advanced reasoning coexists with dyslexia or English development | Combine accessible input/output and advanced intellectual demand | Do not trade challenge away to provide access |
| Executive demands obscure learning | Clarify steps, show a model, externalize planning, and check progress | Distinguish management assistance from a solution to the learning task |
| Group work obscures an individual's understanding | Add a brief explanation, demonstration, or exit response | Capture individual evidence without assuming group product equals individual mastery |

## Classify supports by context
A universal access feature is broadly available; a scaffold is temporary instructional assistance; a documented accommodation implements an identified access arrangement; a modification changes the intended learning expectation. These categories can overlap in practice: cite the applicable plan or policy and explain the learning/measurement effect rather than relying on the label alone.
Do not claim a classroom support is permitted on a formal state assessment without verifying the current administration rules. Do not remove a documented accommodation simply because a student performed well once.

## Text and task adaptation
1. Identify essential ideas, relationships, evidence, domain vocabulary, and the original target.
2. Locate avoidable language, layout, sensory, background-knowledge, or task-management demands.
3. Preserve the original or a supported-original route. When useful, supply a parallel version with chunking, definitions, visuals, or explicit connections.
4. Check factual fidelity, qualifications, examples, logical connections, and cultural assumptions. Name any omission or change that affects the target.
5. Offer a way to engage with the original complexity over time. Do not use a readability number as a claim of validated level or as the sole placement measure.

## Advanced learning and grouping
Preassessment may justify compacting particular material, not an entire domain by identification alone. Propose an extension that requires new reasoning: compare explanations, investigate constraints, generalize, test a counterexample, evaluate evidence, or defend an original design.
Use task-specific groups that can change with student work. Avoid treating all multilingual learners, IEP holders, or gifted-identified students as homogeneous. Let a learner receive both scaffolded access and intellectual extension.
For an acceleration decision, surface evidence and local procedures; do not claim to authorize placement.

## Follow-up examples
- Remove a temporary planning prompt after the learner independently selects relevant evidence on more than one opportunity; keep required access arrangements.
- Extend the task after the learner explains the target concept and applies it to a new example; use a counterexample or constraint rather than extra repetitions.
- Regroup after new work shows that the barrier was vocabulary rather than the underlying concept.
Treat these as proposed rules to tailor, not empirically validated universal cutoffs.

# Flexible grouping and feasibility

[California MTSS](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/cr/ri/mtsscomprti2.asp) links needs, instruction and coordinated support. [NCII DBI](https://intensiveintervention.org/data-based-individualization) and [progress monitoring](https://intensiveintervention.org/data-based-individualization/progress-monitoring) inform target-specific review and adaptation. These sources do not prescribe one universal grouping algorithm. The workflow below is an original planning method, not an eligibility or placement instrument. Sources opened 2026-09-29 UTC.

## Evidence-to-roster protocol

Use one row per student and instructional target, with source/date, actual response, opportunity, response condition and current support. Classify evidence as sufficient for a particular instructional proposal, conflicting, stale, or missing; describe why. A learner may need different peers for different targets. A broad test total may conceal separate decoding, comprehension, language, representation or prerequisite needs.

Propose the next shared teachable step, then examine compatibility of prerequisite readiness and response modes. Give each learner's rationale, not just a group average. Keep extension and maintenance opportunities available. If a low result arose from missing access or no opportunity, repair that evidence gap before calling it a skill deficit. Treat identities and diagnoses as relevant support context only when actually supplied, never as clustering shortcuts.

## Feasibility audit

List discrete periods and protected core/service windows. For each period, enumerate adult availability/competence, rooms/materials and capacity. Assign a learner to at most one concurrent activity and an adult to one supervised group unless an actual approved arrangement supports more. Validate group size against the supplied intervention conditions, required service minutes, transitions, and preparation. Distinguish minutes scheduled from minutes actually delivered.

If constraints conflict, show the exact unsatisfied requirement and a feasible alternative. Example: two adults, one 20-minute block and three groups each requiring direct adult teaching cannot all run concurrently. Propose another block or a reviewed priority schedule; do not assign an unsupported independent group and pretend all intervention minutes are covered.

Use neutral group identifiers and target labels. Keep rosters private. Do not populate public trackers with deficit labels. Proposed participation does not change formal placement or service plans.

## Review and regrouping

For every group choose a fresh task/measure, review window and rule for maintaining, adapting, moving to a different target or reducing temporary support. Rules depend on measure/program evidence and local decisions; a generated percentage threshold is a proposal. Review access, exposure and delivery alongside response. A student who has not received planned instruction has a delivery gap, not demonstrated nonresponse.

Record date, old/new target, evidence and reason for each change. Preserve teacher access. Refer repeated nonresponse or complex needs to the appropriate team rather than generating a disability diagnosis. Offer a diagnostic micro-group or individual check when available evidence cannot support a defensible roster.

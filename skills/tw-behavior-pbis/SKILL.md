# tw-behavior-pbis

Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) every time and [coordination](references/coordination.md) when routing work. Verify sources at use; apply only the foundations relevant to this task. Honor teacher AI preferences and preserve documented supports.


## Inputs
Use grade/setting, observable behavior/routine, learning impact, context, existing school framework and available deidentified evidence. Ask what the student should be able to do, not only what should stop. Preserve supplied IEP/504/BIP supports and official school procedures; do not invent them. Read [frameworks and evidence](references/frameworks-and-evidence.md).

## Workflow
1. Define the situation using observable acts, setting, frequency/duration and opportunities. Separate observations, student/teacher reports and hypotheses about function. Consider task access, language, predictability, adult responses, relationships and environment. Obtain student perspectives respectfully without inferring diagnoses or trauma.
2. Fit the school's chosen framework. Use PBIS for prevention, explicit expectation/routine teaching, positive feedback, supported correction and data-based adjustment within a continuum. Connect to MTSS academic/access supports. SEL skill instruction and restorative repair may complement this work but are not interchangeable or a claim of equivalent evidence.
3. Start with a feasible Tier 1 classroom change when appropriate: define/model/rehearse the expected routine, provide a cue before the difficult transition, offer meaningful participation/choice, acknowledge the specific skill and calmly reteach errors. Produce an actual [expectations matrix](assets/expectations-matrix.md), short teaching script and response examples. Do not reduce PBIS to points or prizes.
4. When targeted supports are warranted by supplied evidence, propose a time-bounded team-coordinated plan with entry/review criteria, dosage/opportunities and fidelity checks. For intensive/persistent or safety-relevant needs, organize an evidence-based team handoff; do not pretend to perform a formal FBA/BIP, establish function from a brief anecdote, or prescribe restrictive practices.
5. Select the actual practices using current relevant evidence, preserving WWC recommendation ratings and grade scope. Provide culturally and linguistically responsive access; review who has opportunities, receives feedback or is referred before interpreting disparities. Use school-approved procedures for immediate safety concerns.
6. Use the [observation record](assets/behavior-observation.csv) to record operational definitions, observation windows and denominators. Review implementation and student outcomes separately. Supply a brief maintain/adapt/intensify/refer decision rule labeled as proposed. Coordinate private student reflection/tracking where useful.

## Deliver
Return a practical support plan with expected skill, antecedent/environment supports, model/rehearsal script, feedback and correction examples, student voice/access options, observation method, review date, sources and any specific team question. Acknowledge uncertainty about function. Keep teacher records separate from student-facing materials.

## Boundaries and examples
No public shame charts, humiliation, withholding basic needs, forced emotional disclosure or automated discipline/placement. Do not fade required accommodations as a reward. Keep legal/disciplinary authority questions with `tw-california-edcode-policy` and authorized school teams.
“Grade 4 transitions take ten minutes” → teach a predictable routine and observe time/opportunities. “Use our PBIS expectations in the sub plan” → preserve the school language and embed a short routine script. “A learner leaves difficult writing work” → examine access, teach a help/break routine as appropriate and treat functional explanations as hypotheses, not diagnoses.

# Standards-based grading policy and evidence

Define the claim a grade is intended to make, then choose evidence and reporting rules. A policy can emphasize current proficiency, accumulated points, consistency or another purpose; those are different interpretations. Do not smuggle a preferred policy into arithmetic. Use supplied local rules and identify proposed changes for review.

## Decisions to make explicit

- Standards/targets and which are essential; how task evidence maps to each, without copying one composite into every tagged standard.
- Score descriptors, minimum relevant opportunities and handling of contradictory/stale evidence.
- Missing, unassessed, incomplete, excused and observed low/zero states; the next opportunity to demonstrate learning.
- Formative practice, work habits and behavior contributions; keep them visible and distinguish them from academic evidence.
- Reassessment eligibility/access, additional learning, equivalent targets/forms, retained history and replacement rule.
- Current-status method, cross-standard rollup, essential-standard gates, coverage denominator, rounding and report-card crosswalk.
- Student/family explanations, moderation, review/appeal route and actual local authority.

These are original policy-design prompts, not a research-validated universal formula. A score history 1, 2, 3, 3 can yield latest 3, highest 3, lower median 2 and mean 2.25. Each rule answers a different question. A mean treats distances as numeric; the others have different recency/consistency implications. No result is the official grade until the applicable policy and evidence are established. Missing entries are not additional zeroes.

## Research and California authority

The [WWC review of Kramer and colleagues' PARLO study](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/94417) was reviewed April 2026 using Standards 4.1. It reports a positive mathematics finding and meets standards with reservations because of high cluster attrition with baseline equivalence. Its setting was principally secondary Algebra/Geometry; it evaluates a specific package of assessment, feedback, reassessment and reporting practices. Do not treat that as endorsement of every standards-based policy, a particular averaging rule or Marzano scale. The review expressly does not represent the full evidence base. Retrieve the original study for claims beyond the reviewed finding.

[California Education Code §49066](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EDC&sectionNum=49066) gives the course teacher responsibility for district grades, subject to its stated exceptions and procedures. It does not require a particular standards-based scheme or authorize this tool to assign final grades. Use a school/district policy as the source for local rules; retrieve other applicable requirements for the actual question. The [Testing Standards](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html) provide a validity/fairness framework, not a ready-made classroom grade policy.

Sources above opened 2026-09-29 UTC. Recheck currency and context at use. Compare multiple relevant primary studies when making broad effectiveness claims; a newer blog, product claim or preprint does not replace appraisal.

## AI-assisted scoring

Honor the teacher's choice. Use actual student work, criteria and evidence locators; label AI suggestions provisional, flag uncertainty and preserve human review. Check current primary research for the subject, grade, task, model/version and supports before performance claims. Agreement on one benchmark is not universal validity, fairness or authorization to post grades. No live gradebook write occurs without explicit authorization.

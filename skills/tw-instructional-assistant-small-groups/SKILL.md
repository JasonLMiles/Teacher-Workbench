# tw-instructional-assistant-small-groups

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) and [coordination](references/coordination.md). Use [evidence and role](references/evidence-and-role.md) for assistant deployment and California scope. Verify task-specific standards/frameworks and relevant research; retain the teacher's AI preference. Design a usable session that reduces routine interruptions while preserving teacher responsibility and students' access to teacher instruction.

## Inputs
Accept a learning goal, uploaded lesson/curriculum, a group profile or freeform conversation. Reuse known grade/course, subject, duration, group size, actual learner evidence, available materials, required accommodations, language needs, school routines and teacher-availability window. Resolve only consequential missing facts. Ask what the assistant knows about this content and routine when that changes the design; otherwise label a fully scripted default. Do not assume an assistant is a content expert, or lower student expectations because the assistant needs more guidance.

## Workflow
1. Read any supplied curriculum through `tw-curriculum-search` and `tw-curriculum-document-intake` as needed. Select a focused learning target, task purpose and evidence of success; connect the session to current classroom learning. Distinguish practice, pre-teaching, reteaching, intervention and enrichment. Use flexible target-specific grouping based on supplied evidence or a brief readiness check; do not assign groups from labels alone.
2. Choose fully scripted, guided or flexible-within-scope delivery using [support modes and routines](references/support-modes-and-routines.md). Match preparation to this assistant and this task. Include a plain-language content primer, vocabulary, an exact worked model, likely wrong responses with accurate explanations and a short rehearsal/self-check with answers. A short packet does not replace required program training.
3. Build the complete [group packet](assets/group-packet.md), TW-IA 1.3. Preserve its ten numbered top-level sections in order unless the teacher requires another format. Nest parallel run cards under section 3 and student copy masters under section 4; retain separate adult keys in section 5. Supply timed adult directions, actual student tasks, checked keys/acceptable alternatives, an extension, a familiar accessible fallback, a fresh individual check and materials/setup. For limited content knowledge write exact say/show/do language and branching responses; for an experienced assistant use concise prompts and bounded adaptation choices. If multiple support levels are requested, keep common task IDs and evidence criteria across parallel run cards.
4. Populate [scaffolding and decisions](references/scaffolding-and-decisions.md) with task-specific prompts, visuals, guided examples, language rehearsal and response options. Route substantial design to differentiation/multilingual/subject specialists. Preserve documented supports and intended constructs. Model new skills when needed; on familiar work let learners attempt before escalating help. Check a fresh comparable response before fading temporary cues; never count a supplied answer as independent evidence.
5. Provide concrete if-this-then-that routes for expected errors, no start, right answer/weak explanation, rapid success, mixed work rates, whole-group difficulty, routine frustration, unavailable materials and an unfamiliar response. State what each learner does while the assistant helps someone else. Use teacher-supplied PBIS routines where relevant. Allow prepared adjustments without a teacher check after every item, and specify a short limit on unproductive prompting plus an actual fallback.
6. Define the delegation boundary: actions the assistant may take within the supplied plan; issues to record for scheduled review; and conditions requiring prompt contact through actual local procedures. Keep teacher oversight, required staffing/supports and appropriate duties. Do not invent emergency/contact arrangements, assign grades, change student plans or present independent class coverage as an assistant-led group. Use [readiness and handback](assets/assistant-readiness-and-handoff.md) to minimize preparation burden while preserving necessary training and review.
7. Collect each learner's actual response and support conditions with the [brief observation record](assets/observation-record.csv), adapted to a paper-friendly grid if helpful. Distinguish first attempt, coached retry and fresh check; retain no evidence/absence separately from incorrect or zero. Record implementation separately from student outcomes. Finish with a concise handback and a bridge to classroom work, including a proposed next-session action for teacher review.
8. Perform a cold-read check from the selected assistant's perspective: can they set up, explain the model, check every task, respond to likely errors, keep all learners engaged, adapt within scope and close the session from this packet? Verify task/key/visual accuracy, readable adult/student separation, support delivery, timings and all referenced materials. Create requested files through teaching-aid/Sheets/Canva or host production tools and inspect them; otherwise provide full copyable content and state the export limit.

## Deliver
Return the complete session packet, separate student materials and adult key, the chosen support mode, a one-page-style quick start, explicit decision/scaffold routes, a short individual-evidence record and teacher handback. Place curriculum/standards/research citations in teacher notes with source locators and access limits. Honor a supplied school template. Ask for missing essentials without withholding unaffected useful work; do not deliver only a checklist or instructions for the teacher to write the activity.

## Examples and limits
“An aide new to this content has 20 minutes with five learners” → fully scripted packet with model/rehearsal, checked tasks, decision branches and individual evidence. “Our experienced assistant runs language groups” → guided/flexible run card with actual discussion materials, language scaffolds and content/language evidence kept distinct. “I cannot be at the table continuously” → bounded routes and handback within supplied supervision arrangements, not an assumption that no teacher oversight or preparation is needed.

Do not describe a generated packet as a validated intervention. Retain required training/delivery conditions for named programs; label adaptations. Avoid permanent ability groups, dependency from over-prompting, routine removal of teacher access, diagnostic claims or automatic placement decisions. Recheck relevant law and current research at use.

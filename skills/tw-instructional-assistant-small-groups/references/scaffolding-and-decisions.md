# Scaffolding, bounded decisions and independent evidence

Populate each support with actual content from the activity. A generic instruction to “provide scaffolding” is incomplete. Retain documented accommodations throughout; these are not temporary hints to withdraw when recording independent reasoning.

## During teaching and guided practice
Offer enough processing time and the least task-specific help that will enable progress, based on observed need. If the concept is new, teach it explicitly using the supplied checked model before asking for application. Never require prolonged failure before giving needed teaching.

| Temporary support | Write into the activity | Evidence to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe / learner self-check | A short checklist or request to explain the plan, with required access supports available. | Does the learner select and execute an appropriate next step? |
| General process prompt | Exact words directing the student to an available strategy without giving the content answer. | Can the learner use the strategy without another adult cue? |
| Content cue or partial example | A precise visual, vocabulary cue, organizer or partially completed step linked to the barrier. | Can the learner complete the next step and explain its purpose? |
| Model and retry | A fully solved analogous example with a short explanation, followed by a different prepared item. | Does the learner transfer the modeled step to the fresh item? |
| Explicit correction, then new application | State the correct feature and why when teaching requires it; supply a fresh application. | Record the taught answer separately from what the learner subsequently produces. |

Return to less temporary assistance on a fresh comparable opportunity after success; do not automatically remove support after a fixed number of items or a timer. Specify the observed evidence for maintaining, adjusting or fading each scaffold. A provided answer, copied model or repeated coached item is not independent evidence. Use a new item/check and record support conditions.

## Decide and act without routine teacher interruption
For the particular content, include a short table with: observed response; likely interpretation labeled tentative; exact next words/action; prepared task to use; record; escalation point. Cover at least:

- Accurate response and explanation: move to the next prepared task or a deeper extension of the same goal.
- Correct answer without an explanation: ask for the evidence/representation specified in the key.
- A predicted error: use the exact correction/model, then a fresh check.
- A student who cannot start or has a language/recording barrier: use the listed visual, oral, chunked or response support; preserve the construct.
- Several students stuck: use the prepared whole-group reteach rather than giving incompatible mini-lessons simultaneously.
- Different work rates: slower learners keep essential practice; quicker learners get a supplied extension, not unbounded extra work or constant peer tutoring.
- Off-task behavior or frustration: use supplied classroom/PBIS routines and a concrete neutral redirection; check task access. Do not invent consequences.
- Missing materials/technology: use a complete offline alternative that preserves the target, or a stated achievable portion.
- An unexpected answer or disagreement with the key: check the worked reasoning, record the student's explanation, avoid calling an unverified alternative wrong, and use the prepared fallback while the teacher reviews.

Set a short, task-appropriate limit on unproductive prompting/reteaching, labeled a proposed operational rule. After that, capture the difficulty and switch to the prewritten accessible review/fallback. Do not repeat the same prompt indefinitely, supply answers to produce apparent success, or interrupt the teacher for every error.

## When to contact the teacher
Distinguish: (1) handle now using the packet, (2) note for the planned review, and (3) contact the teacher/designated staff promptly using the school's actual procedures. A routine misconception normally follows the packet's branch. An unresolved content issue belongs in the handback unless it prevents all prepared work. A concern about safety, significant distress, missing essential support or a task requiring an unavailable trained role may require prompt contact. Do not invent contact names, signals or school emergency procedures; mark missing local routes. No “teacher unavailable” instruction removes those responsibilities.

Define what students safely do while awaiting help using an actual prepared task within their current access/knowledge. Do not make the fallback a new difficult lesson. Do not make school staffing or supervision assumptions beyond the supplied context.

## Observation and handback
Record an anonymous student code, item/task, first response, evidence or work location, temporary help, required supports, retry/check response, and a brief implementation note. Keep no opportunity/absent/not observed distinct from zero or an incorrect attempt. Keep behavior, participation and content evidence distinct. Recording responses against teacher-supplied criteria is for teacher review; it is not assigning student grades.

Finish with three brief findings: what each student showed; what help/material/route was used; what needs teacher attention before the next session. Include a classroom bridge: where the teacher or student can use the same strategy in the next lesson. A short successful group check does not establish long-term intervention effectiveness.

# Assistant-led small-group packet — TW-IA 1.3

Fill this template with actual content; remove unused fields. Keep the quick start compact and place student pages separately from adult scripts/keys. Preserve a required teacher template, mapping these components into it. A narrow revision may return only the affected portion.

## 1. Assignment and quick start
- Grade/course; subject; group purpose; source/edition/locators; current classroom connection.
- Learning target and observable success criteria; relevant verified standards.
- Group size and target-specific grouping rationale from supplied evidence.
- Session duration and schedule; selected assistant support mode and assumption if unknown.
- Materials, setup, preparation/readiness check, and required supports.
- Teacher-approved choices; what remains for teacher review; local contact route if supplied.

## 2. Content primer and rehearsal
Explain only what the assistant needs to teach this activity accurately, in plain language. Define necessary terms. Include a checked worked example and explanation, a common wrong response with a correction, and a short self-check with answers. State the fallback if the assistant is not yet ready.

## 3. Timed run sheet
| Time/minutes | Say/show/do | What every student does | Look/listen for | Next action/task ID |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Write complete directions and account for individual checks, transitions and recording within the total time. Use source task IDs consistently.

## 4. Student tasks and materials
Provide all task cards/text, visuals or exact drawing instructions, prompts and response space. Include guided work, fresh individual check, prepared extension and accessible fallback. Separate these pages from answer keys.

## 5. Adult answer key and response examples
For every task ID include the correct response, worked reasoning or criteria, acceptable alternatives, and likely errors. Check that the key and student version match. Mark any unresolved answer before delivery.

## 6. Scaffolds and language/access supports
| Barrier or observed need | Actual prompt, cue, model or tool | When to use | Fresh check / evidence to adjust | Required support retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 7. If-this-then-that decisions
| Observed response/event | Exact action/words | Prepared next task | What to record | Handle now / review later / contact promptly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include what the group does while one learner gets help and while waiting for any necessary teacher response. Supply content for fallback/extension rather than naming activities that do not exist.

## 8. Individual check and brief record
Protect a fresh individual response with required accommodations; label temporary prompts/modeling. Use one simple row per learner/task, not a lengthy data-entry task during teaching. Supply criteria for shown/not yet shown/no evidence and a work-sample location.

## 9. Teacher handback and next-class connection
Summarize what was taught, what each learner showed, supports actually used, delivery/missing-material issues and questions for the teacher. Identify where the skill will reappear in class. Propose the next-session decision for teacher review; do not assign grades or permanent groups.

## 10. Teacher source/change notes
Cite relevant curriculum locations, California alignment and research claims. Separate supplied facts from original tasks and adaptations. Identify preparation/training or essential local details still required, with a feasible available route. Keep substantive source citations off student response pages where they distract from learning.

# Teacher Workbench Edtech Integration

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: learning goal and current barrier/opportunity. Optional: grade, available/approved tools, devices, access, budget, support conditions and AI preference.

## Workflow
1. Define what students should learn and why current practice needs support. Compare a low/no-technology option before deciding a tool adds learning value.
2. Verify current official product capabilities, pricing/access conditions, age/account requirements, accessibility information and relevant school approval. Do not recommend a purchase or student account on stale information. Minimize student data and distinguish vendor claims from independent findings.
3. Apply only useful frameworks: TPACK for content–pedagogy–technology fit; Triple E for engagement with goals, enhancement and extension; current ISTE Students/Educators/Coaches or Education Leaders indicators as appropriate; computational-thinking competencies when relevant. Check actual indicators and versions.
4. Design the task, teacher model, student interactions, evidence and feedback. Explain the technology's concrete contribution. Use CAST guidance to inspect barriers; a framework label is not evidence of effectiveness.
5. Plan access, preparation, classroom routines, a low-bandwidth/offline equivalent, and teacher review. If AI is involved, preserve the teacher's selected level and load the AI skill for substantial integration.
6. Compare learning evidence, feasibility, workload and equity after use. Keep a tool only if it helps the stated purpose under the actual conditions.

## Deliver
Return a learning-value comparison with verified product facts, a feasible task/implementation plan, relevant framework connections, access checks and an equivalent failure route.

## Limits
Do not invent compliance certification, district approval, product terms or evidence. Do not treat engagement or convenience as proof of improved learning. Never require a tool simply to satisfy a framework.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Would a simulation help this science lesson?” Overlap: generative AI → AI integration with explicit choice. Boundary: no verified approval for student accounts → teacher demonstration or offline route while flagging the unresolved condition.

# Teacher Workbench Mathematics Instruction

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: grade/course and mathematical target or problem. Optional: student methods, representation preferences, prior knowledge, adopted materials, timing and supports.

## Workflow
1. Verify California content and mathematical practice standards and relevant framework guidance. Define the mathematical idea and the evidence of understanding rather than just a page count or procedure.
2. Solve the task independently; verify numerical keys with available calculation tools. Read actual student work before naming a misconception. Distinguish an arithmetic slip, representation issue, language interpretation and conceptual misunderstanding.
3. Connect concrete/contextual, visual, verbal and symbolic representations where they illuminate the same structure. State exactly what corresponds across representations. Avoid decorative manipulatives with no conceptual connection.
4. Build a sequence: accessible launch, model or exploration suited to prerequisites, contrasting worked cases, purposeful questions, practice with variation, and a transfer problem. Include a plausible incorrect method labeled as a teaching example.
5. Use discourse prompts that ask for reasoning, comparison and revision, and provide participation/language supports. Offer depth for demonstrated mastery rather than extra copies of the same exercise.
6. Check with a short new task and an explanation. Record what evidence would support each hypothesis and choose the next task. Keep essential mathematical demand when changing writing or reading load.

## Deliver
Return a verified problem sequence with worked solutions, representation links, questions, likely errors as hypotheses, support/extension routes and a follow-up task.

## Limits
Do not infer enduring ability from speed or one answer. A calculator or formula sheet changes some constructs; explain when it is appropriate. Label constructed student errors as illustrative.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Explain why fraction division works.” Overlap: analyze a class quiz → student-work/CTT. Boundary: IRT parameters from item wording → IRT readiness, not invented difficulty estimates.

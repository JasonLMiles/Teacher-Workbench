# Teacher Workbench Intervention Progress

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: specific learning need, baseline evidence and target. Optional: verified intervention protocol, schedule, resources, previous response, progress measure and documented supports.

## Workflow
1. Define an observable, teachable target from actual evidence; distinguish missing instruction/opportunity, language/access barriers and a skill need. Coordinate with relevant subject and multilingual/differentiation skills.
2. Select evidence-informed components matched to population/setting. If implementing a named intervention, verify the protocol and required conditions; label adaptations. Do not present an original combination as the validated program.
3. Specify baseline, component, dose, group size, materials, responsible role and implementation check. If dose is a local proposal, label it rather than inventing a research-established schedule.
4. Choose a repeated measure aligned to the target and sensitive to change, with comparable forms/conditions. Record supports and missing observations. Set a review date and transparent provisional decision rule; use verified instrument rules if available.
5. Interpret progress together with fidelity, attendance/opportunity, measurement variation and access. Do not intensify or abandon a practice based on one noisy score without examining conditions.
6. Provide exact instruction/practice materials and a progress log. At review, decide continue, adapt, intensify or seek team review, naming the evidence and uncertainty. Preserve required services and documented accommodations.

## Assistant-led small groups
When an instructional assistant will deliver the plan, use `tw-instructional-assistant-small-groups` for an operational packet, preparation check, scaffold/error branches and teacher handback. Preserve any named intervention’s training, dosage and fidelity conditions; distinguish a generated practice session from a validated program and retain teacher access and scheduled progress review.

## Tiered activities and flexible grouping
Use `tw-intervention-activities` to produce actual Tier 1 core scaffolds, Tier 2 supplemental activities or Tier 3 individualized adaptations with models, tasks, keys, error responses and fresh checks. Use `tw-intervention-grouping` to group by target evidence, check staffing/time/support constraints and specify regrouping evidence. Keep unknown or inaccessible assessment evidence separate from demonstrated difficulty. Return activity/group evidence to this skill's longitudinal monitoring cycle; preserve validated program conditions and do not require completion of tiers before an appropriate referral.

## Deliver
Return a targeted plan, usable routine/material, implementation and progress records, proposed or verified decision rule, and a review action with citations and transfer limits.

## Limits
Do not determine disability eligibility, placement or withdrawal of services. Screening/progress data are not diagnoses. Never fabricate validated cutoffs or claim an arbitrary slope guarantees success.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Plan a six-week decoding support cycle from these errors.” Overlap: uncertain language versus content need → multilingual/subject evidence. Boundary: eligibility decision → school team process and bounded observations.

# Teacher Workbench Family Partnership

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: purpose, audience and verified facts to communicate. Optional: preferred language/tone/channel, family input, school procedures, access needs and authorized recipient. Use only necessary information.

## Workflow
1. Clarify the purpose and verify underlying learning/assessment facts. Distinguish actual evidence from hypotheses; check criteria and support conditions before describing progress.
2. Draft in clear respectful language: strength or concrete observation, learning goal, what evidence shows and does not show, proposed support and an invitation for the family's perspective. Avoid deficit labels, blame, jargon or unsupported diagnoses.
3. Honor preferred language and accessible format. Offer translated drafts with key terms/context; flag important nuance for a qualified interpreter where needed. Do not assume language preference or literacy from identity.
4. Explain scores plainly, including scale/criterion and limits. Distinguish a proposed classroom rule from a validated benchmark. Provide a feasible action families may choose without assuming time, internet or materials.
5. Plan reciprocal follow-up: family question/input, agreed action, responsible person, communication preference and review date. Protect confidentiality in group communications.
6. Return a draft. Send only if the user explicitly authorizes sending and an available host tool can resolve the correct recipient and channel. Never claim a message was sent without a successful tool result.

## Deliver
Return a complete ready-to-review message or conference plan, optional translation, clear next step and reciprocal follow-up record. Put research citations in teacher notes rather than cluttering a short family message unless requested.

## Limits
Do not invent family circumstances, consent, contact details or promises of school services. Drafting authorization is not sending authorization. Follow host recipient-resolution rules before any authorized send.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Draft a family note explaining this rubric.” Overlap: uncertain score interpretation → assessment/CTT first. Boundary: “write an email” without send authorization → provide the draft only.

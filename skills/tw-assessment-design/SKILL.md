# Teacher Workbench Assessment Design

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: purpose/decision, construct, grade/course and content. Optional: time, formats, standards, supports, scoring conditions, AI conditions, prior items and intended reporting.

## Workflow
1. Define the intended interpretation/use and what evidence would justify it. Separate constructs from irrelevant reading, writing, motor, language or technology demands. Verify relevant California standards; consult current Testing Standards publication status.
2. Build the blueprint: target, demand, evidence, format, item count/points, time and coverage. Select formats because they reveal the construct. Do not equate difficulty with cognitive complexity or assume a taxonomy guarantees validity.
3. Write original items/tasks and review accuracy, ambiguity, cueing, distractor rationale, accessibility and cultural/contextual demands. Supply complete student directions, conditions, materials and response format.
4. Write the key, acceptable alternatives, partial-credit rules and analytic criteria before finalizing items. Add illustrative responses clearly labeled as authored exemplars and a calibration process where scorer judgment matters.
5. Distinguish accommodations preserving the construct from modifications changing it. Verify documented supports. For AI-assisted products, collect separate independent evidence if independent learning is the intended inference.
6. Review coverage, timing, scoring agreement and likely sources of error. Pilot where feasible. Label classroom thresholds as proposed local rules and explain the rationale; do not call them empirically established cut scores.
7. Plan what a result will change in teaching. Route real response data to student-work or CTT, and only route IRT when a defensible measurement question/design warrants it.

## Cognitive demand, proficiency and growth
Use `tw-blooms-taxonomy` to analyze knowledge and cognitive-process demands from the actual task and scoring evidence, not verbs alone. Use `tw-marzano-grading-rubrics` for original proficiency scales and anchored rubrics, and `tw-standards-based-grading` for reporting, reassessment and missing-evidence policy. Required accommodations do not automatically reduce a proficiency score. Route dated standard histories/domain rollups to `tw-standards-progress-analysis`, matched comparable pre/post scores to `tw-pre-post-growth-analysis`, and weighted-grade arithmetic to `tw-grade-calculations`. These answer different questions from CTT reliability/item analysis or IRT; retain the intended inference and teacher review.

## Deliver
Return a blueprint, complete student form, teacher key/rubric, administration/support conditions, example scoring and review plan with limits on interpretation.

## Limits
Validity concerns evidence for a use, not a permanent label on a test. Alpha alone does not establish validity; completion, one group product or AI-polished work does not prove individual mastery.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Build a grade 8 assessment on linear relationships.” Overlap: integrated project → cross-curricular skill preserves subject reporting. Boundary: estimate item parameters without responses → IRT/data collection rather than invented estimates.

## Brief checks and artifact review
Use `tw-formative-checks` for a short hinge question, exit ticket or misconception probe with actual next-step teaching materials and a follow-up task. A brief check does not establish psychometric calibration or stable placement. For formal quality review, use `tw-pedagogical-review` on actual questions, stimuli and keys with common, subject and formative criteria where applicable; keep wrong answers, invalid interpretations and incomplete evidence visible.

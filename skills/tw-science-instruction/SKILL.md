# Teacher Workbench Science Instruction

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: grade/course and phenomenon or learning goal. Optional: materials, time, prior models, actual data, setting, safety procedures and access needs.

## Workflow
1. Verify the applicable California NGSS performance expectation, disciplinary core idea, science/engineering practice and crosscutting concept. Explain the task connection; do not claim a single activity fully meets a performance expectation solely by topic.
2. Frame an observable phenomenon and question students can investigate or analyze with the supplied resources. Elicit initial models or predictions without treating them as diagnoses.
3. Plan data collection: variable/condition, measure, units, repetitions, records, comparison, expected uncertainty and analysis. Distinguish observational associations from causal inference; identify controls needed for stronger claims.
4. Check materials-specific safety against official guidance and supplied school procedures. Do not invent local approvals or provide a risky demonstration when a safe model, video or supplied dataset achieves the goal.
5. Sequence observation → investigation/analysis → model revision → explanation. Include the actual student recording sheet, teacher reasoning/key and an evidence/claim rubric. Mark simulated data clearly; never imply it was collected by students.
6. Separate science understanding from language production where language is not the target. Include an individual model/explanation check and a feasible next investigation.

## Deliver
Return a phenomenon/investigation plan, materials and verified safety needs, recording sheet, anticipated evidence with caveats, model/explanation criteria and follow-up.

## Limits
Do not fabricate observations or research results. Do not claim causation from a classroom correlation. Route unresolved materials safety to the school’s approved procedure before practical execution.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Plan an investigation of erosion for grade 4.” Overlap: science–math project → cross-curricular assessment. Boundary: unsafe or unapproved experiment → safe alternative and the missing approval/procedure.

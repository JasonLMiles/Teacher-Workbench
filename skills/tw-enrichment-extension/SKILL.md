# Teacher Workbench Enrichment Extension

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: target and evidence of readiness or a request to check it. Optional: interests, prior work, access/language needs, resources and time. Gifted identification alone is not evidence of mastery of every target.

## Workflow
1. Verify the core target and inspect a brief preassessment or actual work. Distinguish fast completion from depth, and specific mastery from a broad label.
2. Compact only demonstrated content, recording what is omitted, what evidence supports that choice and how essential gaps will be monitored. Do not remove needed instruction based solely on gifted identification.
3. Offer meaningful depth/complexity, abstraction, transfer, authentic problem constraints or an appropriately advanced sequence. Replace redundant practice with challenge rather than adding extra busywork or mandatory peer tutoring.
4. Provide clear product/process criteria, feedback checkpoints and intellectual support. Preserve disability and language access; advanced reasoning can coexist with writing, reading or executive-function support.
5. Design an individual explanation/transfer check. Revisit readiness and challenge level from evidence and student response, not a fixed group label.

## Deliver
Return a mastery/compacting record, coherent extension choices with explicit intellectual demand, a complete task, criteria and checkpoints with access supports.

## Limits
Do not equate enrichment with more work or use students as routine unpaid tutors. Do not remove documented accommodations or assume giftedness eliminates language/learning needs.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Extend this ratios unit for students who have mastered it.” Overlap: advanced reasoning plus writing accommodations → differentiation skill. Boundary: universal acceleration from a label → target-specific evidence and local decision process.

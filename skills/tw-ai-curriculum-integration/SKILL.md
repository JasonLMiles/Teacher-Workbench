# Teacher Workbench Ai Curriculum Integration

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: learning goal, grade/course and chosen AI level when integration is requested. Honor an existing choice: none; teacher preparation only; unplugged literacy; teacher demonstration; approved supervised student use. Optional: approved tool, school policy, resources and assessment conditions.

## Workflow
1. Honor the teacher's choice. If unclear, offer the five levels briefly and default to no student AI use while producing useful planning. Do not require student accounts to teach AI literacy.
2. Verify content standards and relevant current ISTE, UNESCO teacher/student competencies and AI4K12 grade-band guidance. Preserve AI4K12's published draft status. These design frameworks are not California legal mandates.
3. Review current primary research and syntheses for the proposed use and age/context. Distinguish assisted output quality from independent learning, retention and transfer. Check study dates/model versions, comparator, duration, null/adverse findings and publication status; newer publication does not mean evidence about the latest model.
4. Define teacher/student/AI roles and the cognitive work students must retain. Design prediction, generation or critique, source verification, explanation, revision and reflection. Include bias/data limitations, uncertainty and human responsibility appropriate to age.
5. Before product-specific student use, verify current provider terms, age/account conditions, data handling, accessibility and actual school approval. Do not invent approval or interpret a vendor badge as legal compliance. Use teacher demonstration or unplugged materials when conditions are unresolved.
6. Provide a complete equivalent non-student-AI route. Require no identifying student information in prompts. Establish clear allowed assistance and simple disclosure/attribution expectations.
7. Assess independent reasoning separately through an explanation or fresh transfer task under stated conditions. Treat AI detector output as insufficient evidence of misconduct. Review actual learning and workload; revise or stop a use that does not serve the goal.

## Deliver
Return an AI-use design canvas, complete activity and example prompt/output-review task, verified framework/evidence notes, non-student-AI equivalent, disclosure guidance and independent assessment.

## Limits
Do not promise AI accuracy, privacy, effectiveness or access on unverified facts. Do not call synthetic examples live tool outputs. Do not claim disclosure automatically establishes originality or use detectors as proof.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Teach students to evaluate AI claims without accounts.” Overlap: an AI-assisted project → assessment design separates assisted product and independent evidence. Boundary: teacher says no AI → preserve that choice and route to ordinary instruction.

# Proficiency scales, task rubrics and grade analysis

The primary [Marzano Resources scale-with-activities reproducible (2020), pp. 1–2](https://soltreemrls3.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/marzanoresources.com/media/documents/reproducibles/HDUPSC/proficiencyscalewithsampleactivities.pdf) illustrates a 0–4 scale: target content at 3, supporting content at 2 and deeper application/inference beyond the target at 4; lower levels describe demonstrated partial performance and instructional assistance. Read the actual local model before using fractional levels. This skill authors original descriptors and examples; it does not reproduce the commercial scale bank or imply endorsement.

## Build the target before the levels

Start from a verified California standard, separate its clauses/constructs and choose a coherent measurement topic. Describe the target in observable evidence terms. Supporting content prepares for that target; extension adds meaningful transfer or depth while target performance remains fully proficient. Do not automatically map Bloom categories to numbers or move a required part of the standard into score 4 merely because it sounds difficult.

A proficiency scale describes a learning progression/topic; a task rubric defines criteria for a particular performance. Map the task criteria back to the scale. Each criterion should name evidence a rater can locate. Avoid “excellent/good/fair/poor” without an operational distinction. Separate content reasoning, language accuracy, presentation and habits unless their combination is justified by the construct and policy.

Use **insufficient evidence/not assessed** outside the performance scale. An observed zero may have a defined meaning; no submission does not establish that meaning. Document required accommodations separately from substantive cues. A learner using authorized speech-to-text or oral response can still demonstrate the target; access support is not automatically the “help” defining a lower proficiency level.

## Original mini-example (synthetic)

For a teacher-defined claim-and-evidence target, a target descriptor might require a defensible claim, two accurate relevant details and an explanation connecting them. A supporting descriptor could show accurate retrieval and a relevant claim but an incomplete evidence link. Extension could reconcile two defensible interpretations and explain limits in a new source. The full task must actually offer that opportunity. These descriptors illustrate the design process; verify the specific California standard and local score meanings before assigning numbers.

Provide anchor responses at the target and adjacent boundaries, with highlighted evidence and a non-example. For .5 levels, supply the exact decision rule/descriptor from policy or mark an original proposal; do not average qualitative categories into pseudo-precision. Raters first score independently, compare evidence, reconcile meanings and revisit the anchors. Agreement statistics require actual ratings and an appropriate design; agreement alone does not establish validity.

## Grade interpretation

Retain evidence by target and date, task opportunity, rater and support conditions. A task total cannot automatically be divided into all tagged standards. Repeated demonstrations can inform a policy-defined current-status decision; report uncertainty and coverage. No universal 0–4-to-letter or 0–4-to-percent conversion exists within this skill. Apply the actual crosswalk or compare explicitly proposed alternatives.

[California Education Code §49066](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EDC&sectionNum=49066) concerns teacher determination of district course grades and specified exceptions/procedures; it does not mandate this scale. [Testing Standards](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html) guide evidence for intended score interpretations; alignment or a published rubric model is not itself a validation study.

# Revised Bloom analysis

Use [Krathwohl (2002), pp. 212–218](https://www.chemnet.edu.au/sites/default/files/files/Blooms_taxonomy_2002_Krathwohl.pdf), an original overview by a revision author. It separates cognitive processes—remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create—from factual, conceptual, procedural and metacognitive knowledge. Categories can overlap; the revision does not require a rigid, universal teaching sequence. The six labels and four knowledge types describe intended learning, not score levels.

## Classify the actual thinking

Read task materials, prior teaching, expected response and success criteria. Identify what must be retrieved, interpreted, executed, related, judged or produced. Consider whether the solution or judgment has already been supplied. Explain the classification using the student's required reasoning; a word such as “analyze,” “design” or “explain” cannot establish demand alone. Mark ambiguity if prior familiarity or task context is unknown.

Separate three views: intended objective, actual task opportunity and observed evidence. An attractive product does not prove the creator evaluated evidence; a polished AI output does not reveal the student's cognitive work. Classify tasks and responses, not children.

The empirical study [Larsen and colleagues (2022)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9087k9x2/qt9087k9x2.pdf) examines assumptions about knowledge/process dimensions and verb-based classification in a biology-education context. Use it to question simplistic labeling; do not generalize one setting into a universal rejection or validation of the framework. Read the relevant study methods/findings when making a substantive empirical claim.

## Create useful alternatives

For a selected target, offer a compact sequence or alternative tasks with exact prompts and expected reasoning. Examples for a source-evidence goal: retrieve a stated count; explain a comparison; apply a familiar evidence rule to a new paragraph; examine competing explanations; judge a claim against explicit criteria; produce a defensible explanation with evidence and limits. These are original task designs, not a rule that six questions must appear in every lesson.

Use knowledge types to expose what is needed: terms/data; relationships/principles; methods and conditions for using them; or monitoring/selecting one's strategies. A two-dimensional map can show gaps without imposing equal weights for every cell. Distinguish cognitive complexity from empirical difficulty and familiarity. Do not create automatic crosswalks to Webb DOK, Marzano proficiency, letter grades or grade levels; each answers a different question and requires its own analysis.

## AI and support

When AI is optional, name learner/tool contributions and evidence of the intended student thinking. Drafting with AI does not by itself demonstrate “create”; checking a tool response can involve evaluation only if criteria, reasoning and evidence are actually required and shown. Verify current AI research before efficacy claims. Treat proposed AI taxonomy revisions as proposals until their validation is established.

Offer scaffolds that improve access while preserving the intended thinking. A source glossary may support analysis; a completed comparison that supplies all relationships can remove it. Preserve documented accommodations and distinguish access support from supplying the answer.

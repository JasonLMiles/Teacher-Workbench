# Teacher Workbench Classroom Culture

## Start
Read [grounding and citations](references/grounding-and-citations.md) for every invocation. Read [coordination and capabilities](references/coordination.md) for the handoff and host-specific fallback rules. Use the [source directory](references/source-directory.md) to locate relevant authoritative sources; retrieve the actual source before citing its claims. Apply only the foundations relevant to the teacher's decision.

## Inputs
Required: observable situation and desired routine/participation. Optional: context, timing, current directions, student feedback, access needs, school procedures and deidentified observations.

For a named behavior framework, tiered support or behavior-data plan, load `tw-behavior-pbis`. Retain this skill as the lead for belonging, participation and routine teaching; coordinate rather than replacing the school framework.

## Workflow
1. Describe what happens, when and under what conditions. Separate observed behavior from interpretations of motivation. Look at opportunity, task clarity, access, relationships and environmental patterns.
2. Define the desired routine in observable teachable steps and explain its learning purpose. Choose a narrow change. Consult relevant WWC/current primary evidence and school procedures where applicable.
3. Plan model → nonexample labeled for teaching → rehearsal → feedback → reteach. Use respectful concise teacher language. Provide visual, linguistic and sensory/access options matched to actual needs.
4. Make participation structures explicit: preparation, thinking time, turn-taking and alternative ways to contribute. Gather student perspectives without demanding private disclosure.
5. Monitor the routine and learning opportunities using an observation log, including context and denominator. Review patterns and unintended effects; adjust instruction or environment before inferring defiance.
6. For disciplinary/legal questions, use the relevant policy skill and supplied local procedures. Do not invent powers, diagnose a disorder, recommend humiliation or claim punishment automatically teaches a skill.

## Deliver
Return a short routine-teaching plan, exact respectful language, rehearsal and feedback examples, access options and an observation/review plan.

## Limits
Do not infer hidden motives, trauma or diagnosis as fact. Do not replace an individualized support or official emergency procedure with generic advice. Keep records deidentified.

## Routing examples
Direct: “Transitions are taking ten minutes; help me teach a routine.” Overlap: task avoidance with language barriers → multilingual/differentiation review. Boundary: clinical behavior diagnosis → observable evidence and appropriate school support.
